<img src="images/grb250818b.png" width="100" align="left">
The Franco-Chinese <a href="https://www.svom.eu">SVOM</a> mission,
launched in 2024, hosts four main instruments optimised for following up
gamma ray bursts.
Our
<a href="http://goto-observatory.org">Gravitational-wave Optical Transient
Observer (GOTO)</a> project is now following up SVOM triggers in addition
to other high-energy satellites, and identified the optical counterpart
to GRB 250818B
(<a href="https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/41406">GOTO25fzq/AT 2025ukm</a>)
in observations taken just over half an hour after the trigger.
</p>
<p>Monash PhD student Sergey Belkin led the followup effort on this
object, gathering and compiling multiband optical/near-IR, X-ray and
radio data covering the afterglow. A likely redshift of z=1.216 derived
from Keck spectroscopy made for
a challenging search for a galaxy host, and ultimately none was found.
Sergey's 
<a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026MNRAS.550g1193B">paper</a>
reporting the exhaustive analysis of this 
event has now been published by MNRAS.
</p>
